STRUCTURE

Two bonds with no adhesive and no solder

The manifold and the microchannel wafer are Si–Si oxide-bonded, and the channel layer is bonded directly to the back of the die in place of TIM and a spreader.

Incoming coolantHeated returnCutaway, schematic, not to scale

FLOW

Even flow to every channel

One inlet, one outlet. Coolant runs down the central trunk, splits into the parallel channels on both sides, merges at the bottom and leaves.

  1. 01

    Inlet

    Coolant enters through the inlet port

  2. 02

    Central split

    It runs down the central trunk and splits to both sides

  3. 03

    Parallel channels

    It crosses the parallel microchannels, picking up heat and turning from blue to amber

  4. 04

    Merge and exit

    The flow merges at the bottom and leaves through the outlet

Why this layout

  • A mirrored U-path keeps flow and pressure drop equal on both sides, so no region runs short and forms a hot spot
  • Parallel microchannels greatly increase the solid–liquid contact area for better convective heat transfer

PACKAGING

Continues through your existing packaging flow

Once bonded to the die, MCL continues through the existing packaging flow. No cold plate is bolted on afterwards.

Conventional

  1. Package the die
  2. Apply TIMBack-end assembly

Apacecore MCL

  1. Bond MCL to the die
  2. Continue packaging
  3. No back-end assembly
  • Fewer interfaces
  • Less assembly tolerance
  • Easier control of integration and yield

SYSTEM

What the system needs

MCL is direct liquid cooling. Unlike air cooling, the system side needs three things.

Pump
Drives the coolant around the loop
Liquid loop
Tubing and fittings carry coolant into MCL and take the heat to a heat exchanger
Leak-proof design
Fittings and tubing for direct liquid cooling need a leak-proof design, planned together with the integration
